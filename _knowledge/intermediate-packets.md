## Summary

An **intermediate packet** is a small, tangible, reusable unit of progress
produced at the end of a short sprint — instead of one large final deliverable.
The idea comes from Tiago Forte's "Just-in-Time PM" series: stage a large
project as a sequence of short, intense sprints, each ending with an
intermediate deliverable (notes, brainstorm, examples, outline, prototype,
draft) that can be picked up again later or reused in another project.

## Definition

- **Intermediate packet** — a tangible, intermediate deliverable produced at the end of a short sprint: a set of notes, a brainstorm, a series of examples, an outline, a prototype, or a draft.
- **Sprint staging** — progress is staged as a series of short, <mark>intense sprints rather than delivered as one lump at the end.</mark>
- **Reuse** — previously built packets can be reused for new projects.

## Why it matters

Small, self-contained pieces make a large project less intimidating, easier to
restart after an interruption, and easier to get early feedback on. They fit
short blocks of time, and saved packets can be reused later, which compounds
value across projects. The framing supports focus and creativity by giving
clear goals, quick feedback, and material that sparks new ideas.

## Practical implications

- **Inference:** For knowledge work, produce an explicit intermediate artifact (note, outline, draft, prototype) per working block rather than only at the end of a project.
- **Inference:** Packet-based workflows compose with a second-brain / LLM knowledge base, where packets act as reusable building blocks or atomic notes.
- **Inference:** The "several loops" framing implies iterative refinement — an early packet is expected to be revised, not final.
